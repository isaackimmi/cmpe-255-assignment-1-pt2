Bayes turns evidence into belief.

Update what you believe when new evidence arrives.

The intuition

Start with a prior belief about a class. Then observe evidence that is more common in one class than the other. Naive Bayes combines those pieces with multiplication; “naive” means it treats features as conditionally independent.

Why can Naive Bayes work despite false independence?

Classification only needs a useful ranking or decision boundary; exact joint probabilities are not always necessary. Correlated features can still produce an effective model, though probabilities may be poorly calibrated.

What is a prior shift?

The class prevalence changes between training and deployment. Likelihoods may remain useful, but posterior predictions should account for the new base rate.

02 / 04 · Model evaluation

A threshold makes a decision.

Confusion matrixROC-AUCCost matrix

Metrics are lenses. The right lens depends on the consequence.

The intuition

A score is not yet a decision. Choose a cutoff and every example becomes a true/false positive/negative. The cutoff trades missed positives (type II errors) against false alarms (type I errors).

precision = TP/(TP+FP)   ·   recall = TP/(TP+FN)precision asks “when I alert, am I right?” · recall asks “did I find the positives?”
↗

Costs change the answer

Here a false negative costs four times a false positive. If missing a positive is expensive, a lower threshold can be rational even when it creates more alerts.

There is no universally “best” threshold.

When is precision more important than recall?

When false positives are costly—for example, expensive manual reviews or disruptive interventions. The answer is a stakeholder cost question, not a universal metric rule.

Why can accuracy mislead?

With a rare positive class, predicting the majority class can look accurate while finding none of the events that matter.

A derivative points uphill.

The intuition

The derivative tells us which direction makes the loss increase. With a safe learning rate, gradient descent moves in the opposite direction toward the minimum; a larger rate can overshoot it.

What happens with a learning rate that is too large?

Updates can overshoot the minimum, oscillate, or diverge. A small rate is safer but may require more iterations.

Why do we need partial derivatives?

A multivariable model has one slope per parameter. The gradient collects these partial derivatives into the direction of steepest local increase.

Backprop is the chain rule at scale.

Forward passLocal derivativesCredit assignment

Trace error backward to learn which parameters mattered.

The intuition

First compute a prediction forward through the graph. Then start with the loss gradient at the output and multiply local derivatives backward. Each parameter receives credit for how it influenced the final error.

ŷ = wx + b   ·   L = ½(ŷ − y)²
∂L/∂w = (∂L/∂ŷ)(∂ŷ/∂w)the chain rule turns one output error into local parameter updates

Why cache forward activations?

The backward pass needs local values such as x and the error. Caching avoids recomputing them and makes the chain-rule products efficient.

What is vanishing gradient?

Repeated multiplication by small local derivatives can make upstream gradients tiny, slowing learning in early layers.
